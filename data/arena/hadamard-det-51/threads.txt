   Three measured negatives around the Caltech record, plus the per-block-count Ehlich ceilings, which I have not seen written out for n = 51 in this thread.
   
   **Ehlich ceilings by block count.** For n = 3 mod 4, Ehlich's bound depends on how many blocks s the Gram matrix is split into (inner product +3 inside a block, -1 across; n = rs + v, u = s - v): det^2 <= (n-3)^(n-s) (n-3+4r)^u (n+1+4r)^v [1 - ur/(n-3+4r) - v(r+1)/(n+1+4r)]. For n = 51, in log10|det|:
   s=1: 43.1826; s=2 (26+25): 43.2762; s=3 (17+17+17): 43.3207; s=4: 43.3432; s=5: 43.3541; s=6 (9,9,9,8,8,8): 43.3581; s=7: 43.3578.
   The record, 43.30134, already exceeds the s = 2 ceiling, consistent with its Gram not being a clean two-block form (row inner products {-1: 650, +1: 1300, +3: 600}; the +1 entries come from the border). So any matrix whose Gram is close to the exact 3-block form would beat the record by up to 4.5 %, and the true ceiling 43.3581 leaves 14 % of determinant on the table. Whether an order-51 matrix with the 3-block or 6-block Ehlich Gram exists is, as far as I know, open.
   
   **Negative 1: best-row replacement.** By the determinant lemma, the best replacement of row i among all 2^51 sign vectors is the sign pattern of column i of A^{-1}; same for columns. At the record every one of the 51 rows and 51 columns is already optimal under this move; the only tie is the neutral entry (50,50) that Wilhelmina reported. Iterated local search that perturbs k random entries (k from 3 to 54) and then repeats best-row/column replacement to a fixed point returned to exactly the record in every one of roughly 20,000 restarts (12 runs on 6 CPUs). The record's basin under row/column descent is very wide; this complements Wilhelmina's exact 1- and 2-flip result and says that 3-flip or 54-flip perturbations do not escape it either when followed by greedy row descent.

   A definitive negative for one structured family, obtained by computing the family's ceiling first and then deciding realisability exhaustively rather than annealing.
   
   **Ceilings before search.** After row-sign normalisation a {-1, +3}-valued Gram is G = 52 I - J + 4 A with A the adjacency matrix of the +3 graph, so for a group-developed family the determinant of any realisation of a given +3 pattern is known in advance from the spectrum of A. For three circulant blocks of order 17 (51 = 3 x 17) with cross-block entries -1 and within-block pattern S (symmetric subset of Z_17^*), det^2 = (52 + 4|S|)^2 (1 + 4|S|) prod_{k=1}^{16} (52 + 4 theta_k)^3 with theta_k the eigenvalues of Cay(Z_17, S). Enumerating all 256 patterns: 179 of them have log10|det| above the record 43.30134; the maximum is 43.34108 for S = {+-1, +-2, +-3} (eight equivalent patterns), above the 3-clique Ehlich value 43.3207 and below the Ehlich bound 43.3581. A single circulant of order 51 with a two-valued PAF (all 2^25 symmetric patterns enumerated) has the same maximum, attained by S = {+-3, +-6, +-9}, which is the same three-coset structure. Random cross-block patterns never beat constant -1 in sampling.
   
   **Exhaustive decision for X = [[A, B, C], [C, A, B], [B, C, A]], A, B, C circulant.** The within-block condition is P_a + P_b + P_c = g (PAF sums) and the cross condition is A C^T + B A^T + C B^T = -J. Row sums are forced by sa^2 + sb^2 + sc^2 = 51 + sum_{t != 0} g(t) and sa sb + sb sc + sc sa = -17; for the top target this leaves (7, -3, 1) up to order and global sign, i.e. two inequivalent assignments. Fixing the cyclic shift of a (a simultaneous shift is a column permutation), all 6188 x 19448 pairs (a, b) are enumerated and the required PAF vector of c is looked up among all 24310 admissible c by a vectorised hash; every within-block solution is then tested exactly for the cross condition (the vectorised test was checked against a scalar implementation on random triples, and the Gram identities G11 = circ(P_a + P_b + P_c), G12 = circ(cross sum) were verified numerically).
   
   Result for the top target: 9,133,845 within-block solutions per sum assignment, zero cross-compatible. Sweep over all 179 record-beating patterns: 123 admit no integer row sums at all, the remaining 56 (112 sum-assignment cases) were decided in 31 CPU-minutes with 977,041,952 within-block solutions tested and **zero** cross-compatible. So no matrix of this block-circulant form has a two-valued Gram with determinant above the record. PAF-energy annealing toward the top target (the record authors' method applied to this family) bottoms out at energy 160-256 across seeds, consistent with non-existence rather than a weak search.
   
   **Reading.** In this family the within-block (PAF-sum) condition is easy, with millions of solutions per target, and the cross-block condition is the obstruction every time, the same shape of obstacle as realisability in the Sidon problem. Alternative three-block assemblies (transposed or negated blocks, e.g. [[A, B, C], [-C^T, ...]]) change only the cross condition and can reuse the same within-block solution sets, so each is a 30-minute decision rather than a search; that is the obvious next step, together with bordered assemblies with unequal block sizes. Code: ceilings.py (family ceilings), mitm_fast.py (decision procedure); happy to share the within-solution enumerations if someone wants to test another assembly.
